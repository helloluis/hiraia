# Rotation design decisions — 28 September 2026

The first incoming-Grade-4 blueprint fails at the second assessment. Four of its
six slots have just one selected item; those four must repeat, exceeding the
limit of two exact repeats from the preceding three assessments. More questions
elsewhere in the bank do not fix this specific failure.

The original proposal and failure remain in `blueprints.json` and the pool report.
Revision 2 is a separate **unapproved content proposal**. No child has taken either
form, so this is a design revision before data collection, not a change to a
learner's comparison scale midstream.

Two original slots were unnecessarily tied to single examples: frog back legs
and roots taking up water. Revision 2 defines the stable assessed content as:

- Matching a familiar animal's movement with the body part enabling it.
- Matching an ordinary plant part with its basic function.

These are broader than the original facts. Each item keeps its own narrow
knowledge claim and knowledge-family ID. Correctly answering the fish item does
not demonstrate knowledge of the frog item. A score across these slots is a
sample of the specified content, not mastery of every example or proof of a
practical competency. The candidate lists are explicit and frozen within a
comparison period. Future changes need a new blueprint version.

The other four slots retain their original content. Reuse has distinct examples
involving a jar, paper, clothing and a bag. Sound production has a bell, guitar,
drum and flute. The rubber-band and Sun-source items remain exact recurring
anchors in this small proposal. That uses the entire two-repeat allowance;
the other ten items must therefore avoid the preceding three full assessments.
Two deliberately repeated anchors are not independent evidence of generalized
learning. Report exact-repeat exposure alongside scores and retain every attempt.

The flute item asks which material vibrates, whereas the guitar and drum items
ask how the material moves. Some body-part questions reverse the direction of
the function relationship. Comparable difficulty is **unvalidated**. Review and
pilot data may require different candidates; structural rotation success must
not be reported as equivalent-form validation.

The full-form simulator is a content-planning instrument. It may use drafts only
in an explicitly labelled sandbox mode. It does not approve any item, change
production eligibility, read a real child's history, or implement the app.
Scenarios explicitly state synthetic curriculum confirmation and card-knowledge
exposure. A current date or material grade never supplies missing evidence.

For a follow-up, try six benchmark items and six questions linked to knowledge
encountered in the last fourteen days. If fewer recent families are available,
the draft fallback fills unused places with eligible prior-grade readiness
probes and labels them **readiness supplements**. They are not counted as recent
retention, and the report gives the actual block sizes. If twelve distinct
eligible items cannot be assembled under the repeat limits, defer the session;
do not repeat an entire paper, silently change the benchmark, or introduce
uncovered current-grade material.

No six-item block can establish formal grade placement or prove that Hiraia
caused an improvement. Repeated observations can produce an app-scoped signal
for a teacher to corroborate. All selected questions remain pending language,
teacher, cohort and empirical review.
