# Peru / Quechua: preliminary expansion research

Research date: **30 September 2026**, Asia/Manila. School-material collection updated **1 October 2026**. This is a feasibility brief, not a completed curriculum alignment, corpus audit, or native-language review. It does not change the internationalization work underway elsewhere.

**Assessment:** Hiraia has a useful science-content foundation for Peru. The larger unknown is producing reliable tutoring in a particular Quechua variety. Public data justifies a measured continued-pretraining (CPT) experiment; it does not yet justify budgeting hundreds of millions of clean, distinct, locally appropriate Quechua tokens. Start with one community, one variety, and Spanish alongside it.

Exact corpus observations, units, source URLs, and selected source-file hashes are in [the accompanying inventory](peru-quechua-corpus-inventory-20260930.json). Counts from different rows must not be added.

**Follow-on work, 1 October:** the [primary science target map](PERU-SCIENCE-COMPETENCY-MAP.md) now records all 103 grade performance statements and preliminary Philippine reuse candidates; Peru content alignment remains unapproved. The [country bootstrap recipe](COUNTRY-BOOTSTRAP.md) carries the reusable process forward, and the [reference archive plan](REFERENCE-ARCHIVE.md) covers durable storage of originals and provenance.

## Population and the language decision

Peru's Ministry of Culture reports **3,805,531 people who learned Quechua in childhood**, using its 2017-census reference. Quechua is a language family: central, northern/Amazonian, and southern varieties must not be treated as interchangeable. Two southern starting points are Chanka (Ayacucho, Huancavelica, parts of Apurímac) and Collao (including Cusco and Puno). These are educational/geographic labels, not a perfect one-to-one mapping to dataset codes. [Ministry of Culture, BDPI](https://bdpi.cultura.gob.pe/lenguas/quechua).

**2017 is not the latest census.** INEI has published 2025 results: its 2026 release reports 17.2% Quechua ethnic self-identification. That is a different measure from childhood language, and neither figure counts primary-school learners. This research did not verify an updated Quechua-specific language total. Do not multiply the ethnic percentage by the entire national population to invent a speaker count. [INEI 2025-census results](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/inei/noticias/1417313-censos-nacionales-2025-lima-metropolitana-continua-siendo-la-provincia-mas-poblada).

In May 2026, MINEDU reported over 1.2 million EIB students, 27,041 institutions, and 105,000 bilingual teachers. Those figures cover **all indigenous languages and initial, primary, and secondary education**, not the Quechua primary-school audience. [MINEDU release](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minedu/noticias/1398137-mas-de-1-2-millones-de-estudiantes-aprenden-en-su-lengua-originaria).

For a usable audience estimate, join the EIB institution register to school enrolment data, filtering primary level, active status, region, variety, and teaching-language scenario. The current [MINEDU institution registers](https://minedu.gob.pe/reforma-magisterial/padrones-instituciones-educativas.php) and [EIB register published through ESCALE](https://escale.minedu.gob.pe/documents/10156/5648449/RVM_154-2025-minedu.pdf?version=1.0) are starting points. School counts, enrolments, ethnicity, and language proficiency require separate denominators.

**Pilot recommendation:** choose Chanka or a specified Collao variety based on an actual school/teacher partnership. Availability of an Ayacucho-labelled web corpus alone is insufficient reason to choose Chanka. Do not assume that a child with Quechua heritage is Quechua-dominant, rural, or monolingual.

## Their DepEd and educational approach

The equivalent is **Ministerio de Educación — MINEDU**. Indigenous bilingual education is handled by **DEIB**, the Dirección de Educación Intercultural Bilingüe, within **DIGEIBIRA**. Regional education authorities (DRE/GRE) and local education management units (**UGEL**) are practical implementation partners. [MINEDU's institutional characterization process](https://www.gob.pe/41377-educacion-intercultural-bilingue-proceso-de-caracterizacion-de-instituciones-educativas-interculturales).

Quechua-speaking children follow the national curriculum through **Educación Intercultural Bilingüe (EIB)**. MINEDU's current explanatory material distinguishes strengthening an indigenous first language with Spanish as a second language, and revitalizing an indigenous heritage language where Spanish is dominant. Teaching should connect school knowledge to the child's community and language situation. Older descriptions enumerate three service forms, including urban EIB; do not mix those historical labels with the current page's two-mode explanation. [MINEDU service model](https://www.gob.pe/39448-educacion-intercultural-bilingue-eib-modelo-de-servicio-educativo-intercultural-bilingue-mseib).

For Hiraia, this means an explicit **Quechua–Spanish** learning design. English may remain useful internally, but is not the natural second classroom language here. Ask local teachers which concepts are taught using Quechua terms, Spanish terms, or both; do not fabricate terminology by translating the Philippine glossary mechanically.

Community knowledge is a starting point for questions and investigations. A tutor can respectfully distinguish cultural accounts, observations, and explanations supported by evidence. Hiraia's factual-accuracy rule still applies. A proposed lesson might compare seed germination, soil moisture, or insulation using locally available materials, with the community choosing relevant examples.

## Curriculum and distance from Hiraia

The national framework is **CNEB**, the Currículo Nacional de la Educación Básica. Primary education has six grades, grouped into cycles III (1–2), IV (3–4), and V (5–6). The science area is **Ciencia y Tecnología**, with three competencies: scientific investigation; explaining the physical world; and designing, building, and testing technological solutions. Standards describe end-of-cycle performance, supplemented by grade-specific expectations. [MINEDU primary programme publication](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minedu/informes-publicaciones/6790803-programa-curricular-de-educacion-primaria).

The linked programme is the March 2017 edition, republished in the current MINEDU catalogue; the catalogue date is not a new curriculum edition. Its science section is printed pp. 271–301. Early grades emphasize observation and everyday phenomena; later grades expect evidence, controlled investigations, and iterative construction. Grade 5 explicitly includes animal/plant cells and a particle account of matter; Grade 6 includes molecular motion and temperature. [Official PDF, especially pp. 280–291 and 300–301](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/10209521/6790803-programa-curricular-de-educacion-primaria.pdf?v=1782418171).

The comparison below uses the **repository's** MATATAG baseline, not a claim that Hiraia represents every current Philippine policy. Its [completed scope report](GRADES3-10-CURRICULUM-SUMMARY.md) records 324 competencies across 308 lessons in Grades 3–10. Grades 3–6 account for 144 competencies and 128 lessons. Existing work includes guided activities, practical explanations, and quizzes; it is more than a fact feed, but does not certify hands-on mastery.

| Dimension | Existing foundation | Work required for Peru |
|---|---|---|
| Science concepts | Matter, energy, organisms, ecosystems, Earth, weather, and investigation recur throughout the bank. | Map each Peruvian expectation to specific cards, activities, and assessments. Broad thematic overlap is promising; a reuse percentage is not established. |
| Grade sequence | Philippine Grades 3–10 with grade/quarter ordering. | Author an early-primary pathway for Grades 1–2. Remap later material by Peruvian expectations rather than copying Philippine grade numbers. |
| Concrete sequence mismatch | Plant/animal cells and unicellular organisms are explicitly in Grade 7 quarter 2 in the repository. | Adapt those resources for Peru's earlier introduction; vocabulary and depth must be reviewed for younger readers. |
| What counts as achievement | Guided activities and in-app questions already exist. | Verify coverage of planning an investigation, recording evidence, explaining findings, building a solution, testing it, and improving it. A correct quiz answer does not demonstrate all these performances. |
| Local setting | Philippine species, weather, examples, and classroom vocabulary. | Review illustrations, seasons, local ecosystems, crops, hazards, and household materials with teachers in the selected region. There is no single setting shared by every Quechua community. |
| Language and literacy | Tagalog/Cebuano/English; Grade 5 reading target. | Quechua variety plus Spanish; substantially simpler, potentially oral-first interaction for Grades 1–2. |
| Lesson organization | Philippine quarter ordering and calendar assumptions. | Support locally planned units, school schedules, and community context. National end-of-cycle standards do not establish a universal week-by-week lesson order. |

Local mapping evidence: [elementary competencies](../rag/sources/curriculum-guides/matatag-elementary-competencies.json), [secondary competencies](../rag/sources/curriculum-guides/matatag-jhs-competencies.json), and [curriculum feed design](CURRICULUM-FEED.md).

**An accuracy defect worth retaining:** the official programme's Grade 6 example on printed p. 291 says bacteria need a host to perform their basic functions. That is false as a general statement: many bacteria are free-living. The text was checked against the rendered PDF page, not just OCR. Curriculum authority determines learning expectations; it does not make every scientific example correct. [Microbiology Society's account of bacterial habitats](https://microbiologysociety.org/why-microbiology-matters/what-is-microbiology/what-are-bacteria.html).

The [HPLT manifest](https://data.hplt-project.org/three/sorted/manifest.json) is JSON Lines despite its `.json` extension. The [Quechua download map](https://data.hplt-project.org/three/sorted/quy_Latn.map) points to actual externally hosted shards; the empty HF file viewer does not mean this dataset is unavailable.

**The 42.77M-token figure is not a clean Quechua budget.** The publisher's [12 sample documents](https://data.hplt-project.org/three/sorted/quy_Latn.json), two per quality-score bin, include Spanish passages appended to Quechua, English translation-site boilerplate, religious material, and heterogeneous spellings. These are nonrandom samples: they establish problems exist, not their prevalence. A qualified reviewer must establish variety labels and human-written language quality. Higher generic web-quality scores do not necessarily mean more suitable school language.

Other leads must stay outside the additive total:

- HPLT 2.0 reports 17.31M whitespace words in its cleaned `quy_Latn` slice. It overlaps the newer release; it is not an independent addition. [Release statistics](https://hplt-project.org/datasets/v2.0).
- DCAD-2000 has small `quy_Latn` and `quz_Latn` payloads from named upstream sources, including FineWeb. Treat it as another view of source material until cross-corpus deduplication proves an increment. [Repository](https://huggingface.co/datasets/openbmb/DCAD-2000).
- A newer [Ixari dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpceanAI/Ixari) claims 500M Quechua tokens. The inspected repository tree contained only `.gitattributes` and `README.md`; no usable payload was verified. **Excluded from the available-data estimate.**
- Speech is a separate opportunity: a 2026 paper reports 66 hours of Puno Quechua, of which 36 hours have manually validated transcripts. Audio hours are not CPT text tokens. [Community-led Puno corpus paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.americasnlp-6.13/). Existing [IWSLT Quechua resources](https://github.com/johneortega/IWSLT2026_Quechua_data) also require source-specific review; the repository declares CC-BY-NC-ND terms.
- FLORES and AmericasNLP evaluation material should remain held out. Synthetic instruction data can be assessed separately for SFT; it is not additional naturally occurring Quechua prose.

**Working scale assessment:** several million words of established text and a larger web pool reported at tens of millions of model tokens are publicly identifiable. The distinct, correctly labelled, permission-compatible, useful portion remains unmeasured. There is no verified billion-token Quechua corpus here. Packaging licences and access alone do not establish permission for every underlying work; record provenance and applicable terms before inclusion.

For scale, Hiraia's [consolidation report](../finetuning/cpt/CONSOLIDATION-REPORT.json) estimates **4.309B distinct Tagalog tokens** and **178.463M distinct Cebuano tokens**. Its [24.78B training mix](../finetuning/cpt/FULL-MIX-README.md) includes repetitions and English/Chinese anchors. It is not 24.78B distinct Philippine-language text. Tokenizers differ between these estimates and HPLT, so a precise ratio would mislead.

## School-material collection — 1 October 2026

**Completed acquisition:** 628/628 linked PDFs downloaded; **57,997 PDF pages**, **12,824,862,846 PDF bytes** (12.82 GB decimal), and **4,566,122 raw whitespace words across all languages**. All 628 files have different SHA-256 hashes. 612 have nonzero text extraction; **16 need OCR**. Exact page-text deduplication after whitespace folding reduces the measured words to **4,015,123**. This still includes Spanish, front matter, exercises and near-duplicate editions; it is not a count of clean Quechua tokens.

The [searchable local catalogue](../tools/quechua-school-corpus/index.html) links to the saved PDFs and extracted text. Quality limits established by this collection:

- **Dates and grades can be wrong in mirror metadata.** The Northern pedagogical vocabulary's mirror button says 2025, while its imprint states first edition/printing in 2024. A Kichwa mathematics listing contradicts itself between Grade 1 and Grade 2; both candidates are retained for review. Do not use a mirror upload or allocation label as an edition date.
- **A different PDF is not necessarily new prose.** Exact PDF hashes, exact extracted-document matches, and whitespace-only page-text overlap are reported separately. The [edition-overlap list](../tools/quechua-school-corpus/edition-overlap-review.csv) flags candidate pairs without silently merging them. More thorough near-duplicate comparison is still needed. These school texts may also occur in QuBERT or web corpora; do not add this collection to those totals without cross-corpus deduplication.
- **Zero extracted words do not mean an empty book.** The [OCR queue](../tools/quechua-school-corpus/ocr-needed.json) lists PDFs with no extractable text. A rendered Inkawasi-Kañaris workbook page visibly contains text despite zero extracted words and no listed PDF fonts. These copies require OCR and language review. Conversely, a sparse Kichwa picture dictionary genuinely has few words per page. [Reproducible spot checks](../tools/quechua-school-corpus/manual-checks.json).
- **Public distribution does not clear CPT rights.** Some imprints explicitly reserve reproduction rights. All collected resources remain `training_approved: false`; underlying permission, scientific accuracy, and native-language quality are unverified. Searchable extracted text is an acquisition result, not a clean training manifest.

To reproduce or resume acquisition, run `python3 tools/quechua-school-corpus/collect.py` with `discover`, `resolve`, `download`, then `export`. Run `python3 tools/quechua-school-corpus/audit.py` to verify hashes and page totals and rebuild the local browsing index. The collector uses Python 3.11+ standard-library modules plus Poppler (`pdfinfo`, `pdftotext`); download failures are recorded and resumable. Preserve diacritics and apostrophes: the overlap audit folds whitespace only and never rewrites source text.

## What should be established before a training decision

1. **Choose the actual language setting.** One pilot region, a specific variety, and paid local EIB teacher/language reviewers. Establish children's Quechua and Spanish proficiency, literacy, terminology, and available devices.
2. **Complete a bounded curriculum crosswalk.** Start with one grade and one unit. For each Peruvian expectation, identify reusable Hiraia facts, required local examples, activity evidence, illustration changes, and gaps. Review science independently of translation.
3. **Audit the real text pool.** Preserve original files and provenance. Separate varieties and source domains; remove navigation and unwanted duplicates without erasing meaningful apostrophes, diacritics, or orthographic distinctions. Measure cross-corpus overlap, human-written versus synthetic material, permissions, and retained tokens with the candidate model's tokenizer. Evaluate language identification with native-labelled controls, reporting precision and recall separately.
4. **Run a small, comparative CPT/SFT study only after that audit.** Compare an appropriate Spanish-capable multilingual base with and without Quechua adaptation, using matched grounding and a held-out bilingual science benchmark. Measure factual correctness, comprehension, variety fidelity, tokenization efficiency, Spanish retention, and phone latency. Do not assume the existing Southeast-Asian model is the best base; do not assume a newer multilingual base already tutors reliably in Quechua.

CPT can improve familiarity with morphology and prose; it does not by itself create faithful science teaching or a child's conversational register. Reviewed educational text, bilingual supervision, grounded answers, and independent native evaluation are separate needs. Repeating a small corpus increases training exposure, not the amount of knowledge available.

Still unresolved: the current Quechua primary-school audience by region/variety; a full competency crosswalk; language-filtered MINEDU yields, remaining OCR and acquisition gaps, and reuse terms; the clean unique token yield; and demonstrated tutoring quality on a suitable on-device base. Those are the next research deliverables, not facts this brief assumes.
