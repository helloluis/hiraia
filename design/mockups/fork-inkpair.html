Two printed
inksFederal Blue / Dark Ochre Fork proposal · key = inkpair

The branches are not two verdicts, they are two inks off the same 1950s press. Identical stock, identical key plate, identical cream letter chip — the only thing that changes between A and B is the ink laid down. Blue-and-ochre is a real two-colour schoolbook job, and no printer ever meant one plate as "correct".

Why this reads "different", not "right / wrong"

Blue and ochre have no verdict grammar. Stop/go, tick/cross, marked-up-in-red — every one of those conventions is built on red against green, so removing both hues removes the whole reflex; what is left is a hue difference some 164° wide that the eye reads as elsewhere, not as better.

And the two are made deliberately equal in rank. They share the cream stock, the graphite key plate and an identical cream letter chip, they sit at the same size on the same ledge, and neither is the gold of the ordinary "next" ticket — so nothing marks either one as the default you were already heading for.

Method, collisions, and the honest weak point

  • How the simulations were made Brettel–Viénot–Mollon 1997 dichromat projection at severity 1.0, using the precomputed sRGB half-plane matrices from libDaltonLens: sRGB → linear, pick the half-plane by the sign of lin · n, apply that plane's 3×3, back to sRGB. Sanity-checked against the textbook cases (pure red → #A48B00 under deutan, pure blue → #006087 under tritan). Distances are CIEDE2000 in CIELAB/D65. The three simulated hexes are pasted in as solid colours on four separate frames — there is no CSS filter anywhere in this file, so the cream stock, the peach mat and the engraving render identically in all four and the only thing you are comparing is the ink.
  • Separation holds everywhere ΔE00 between the two inks: 41.8 normal, 43.4 deuteranopia, 42.9 protanopia, 39.3 tritanopia — the worst case is still ~4× the ~10 "too close" floor. Compare the pair being replaced: brick/teal fell to 16.9 under protanopia, where both chips converge on a muddy olive-grey (#6B5F36 vs #5F5F60). Blue-vs-yellow is the one axis that survives all three deficiencies, which is exactly why the pair is built on it.
  • Contrast, on a cheap panel in daylight #F4EAD5 at 17px bold: 7.21:1 on the blue, 5.09:1 on the ochre, 7.67:1 on the graphite banner. All clear 4.5:1; blue and graphite clear AAA. Worth recording that the brick being replaced measured 4.33:1 and never actually passed AA.
  • No collision with the gold ticket Ochre #7A5E22 sits ΔE00 27.2 from gold #D8A03A (blue is 59.1) — the gap is carried almost entirely by lightness, L* 41.6 against L* 69.5. Held against the rest of the palette: A/B vs forest ink 28.1 / 29.1, vs board 26.1 / 29.1, vs olive 33.6 / 19.3.
  • Teal keeps its job #2F6360 is no longer a fork colour; it goes back to being the quiz stock and nothing else, which is cleaner than the old scheme where the quiz card and choice B were the same ink. They never share a screen, but for the record: blue vs teal ΔE00 23.5, ochre vs teal 29.3.
  • The neutral banner — the key plate SANGANDAAN, its rule and the fork keyline were all brick, i.e. literally choice A's colour, which tied the announcement to one branch. They become press graphite #4D4740: the black plate every two-colour job also runs, belonging to neither ink (ΔE00 23.7 from blue, 19.0 from ochre) and warm and light enough (L* 30.5, C* 5.2) to stay distinct from the forest ink used on ordinary bands.
  • Still mid-century, still on-brand A dusty federal blue against a bronzed ochre, both under C* 40, is the standard two-colour schoolbook job of the period — and against the cat's forest outlines, olive tabby and golden eyes it lands as the complementary cool the palette never had. Nothing here is a gradient, a blend mode or a shadow: six background colours and two border colours, all RN backgroundColor / borderColor, so the port is free.
  • What I tested and rejected The other period-plausible ink pairs collapse for dichromats and the numbers are brutal. Payne's grey #4C5866 + dusty violet #6B5578: ΔE00 4.6 deutan, 6.2 protan — effectively the same swatch twice. Federal blue + mulberry #6E4A63: 13.7 / 9.3. Federal blue + aubergine #573A5E: 8.0 / 6.3. Any warm second ink that is not red must be yellow, which is what forces ochre.
  • The honest weak point Ochre is gold's own hue family. Requirement 1 bans red and green, and pushing the ochre's hue further from gold's 75° runs either into orange (reads red) or into yellow-green (reads green), so h 83° is forced — and the ochre is only separated from the gold ticket by lightness, not by hue. ΔE00 27.2 is comfortably past the 15 floor, and on the fork card gold only survives as the small number chip and the brass rings, so the two never sit edge to edge at size. But on a sun-washed 720p panel with the brightness turned down, a squinting child could plausibly read choice B as "the dark gold one" — and that is a nudge toward B, which is the exact failure mode this brief exists to remove. If that shows up in testing, the fix is to darken the ochre toward L* 36 (ΔE to gold rises past 33) and pay for it with contrast headroom, which there is plenty of.
