Why this reads “different”, not “right vs wrong”

Valence in this palette is carried by temperature, not by hue distance: warm advances and warns, cool sits still. Blueprint blue and dusty mulberry are both cool — both have a negative CIELAB b* — so there is no warm one for the eye to nominate as the alert, and no cool one left over to read as the safe default. What separates them is 85° of hue, which the eye reads as two different subjects, the way two coloured inks on a school map mean two different things rather than a good one and a bad one.

The banner no longer takes a side. It was brick — the same colour as choice A — which quietly endorsed A. It is now olive drab, pulled from the cat’s own tabby coat: the character who announces the fork is the one whose colour frames it, and that colour appears in neither ticket.

Method, and where this pair is weakest

  • How the simulations were made Brettel–Viénot–Mollon (1997/1999) dichromat projection: sRGB → linear RGB → LMS, projected onto the half-plane the missing cone collapses to, then back. Applied to the three changed colours only and written straight into the panels as solid hexes — there is no filter anywhere in this file, so the cream, the ink, the brass and the engraving are identical in all four frames and the only thing you are comparing is the thing that changed. Distances are CIEDE2000; ΔE₀₀ below ~10 would be too close.
  • Why lightness does the heavy lifting Blue and violet do converge in hue under protanopia — that is the real risk of a two-cool pair. So the pair is separated on a second axis as well: A sits at L* 44.4 and B at L* 35.4, and protanopia widens that gap to ΔL* 13.2, because the mulberry’s long-wavelength content loses luminance while the blue keeps it. The blue also stays visibly chromatic (#496CA5) while the mulberry desaturates to slate (#454C5C): saturated-vs-neutral, not just light-vs-dark.
  • What the incumbent actually did Brick #B24F35 vs teal #2F6360 under protanopia becomes #6B6036 vs #5E5F60 — ΔL* 0.6. Two muddy mid-tones at the same lightness, distinguished only by a residual tint. Brick also fails AA outright on its own label: 4.33:1, under the 4.5 floor. Both new colours clear it.
  • Still mid-century? Checked by chroma, not vibes. Blueprint blue is C* 36 and mulberry C* 27 — both less saturated than the brick they replace (C* 52) and than the gold that stays (C* 60); mulberry sits right beside the peach mat (C* 31). This is 1950s wall-map blue and 1950s dusty plum, not 2020s indigo-and-violet. Nothing neon, nothing pastel.
  • Weakest point — protanopia, 16.5 Protanopia is the binding case at ΔE₀₀ 16.5. That is a comfortable pass, but it is the smallest number in the set and it is the price of refusing to use temperature as the separator. Note that CIEDE2000 flatters the incumbent here (17.2) by ignoring that its two colours land at the same lightness; on ΔE₇₆ this pair reads 27.7 against the incumbent’s 26.3, and on ΔL* it is 13.2 against 0.6.
  • Honest caveat — mulberry goes grey for deuteranopes Under deuteranopia choice B lands on #55575B, a true neutral. It stays unmistakably separate from choice A, which is the requirement — but a grey ticket can read as disabled rather than as the other option. Mitigated by the fact that both tickets keep an identical cream letter chip, cream label, chevron and ink ledge, so nothing about B’s form says inactive. Worth one look on a real device; if it reads dead, the fix is to push B toward #7A4460 (more chroma), not to change its hue.
  • Honest caveat — tritanopia re-warms choice B Tritanopia turns A cyan (#00748C) and B a muted brick (#75464C), so for a tritanope the pair does regain a warm/cool split and B could read faintly as the “alert”. Separation is by far the best of the three (ΔE₀₀ 46.1) so nothing is lost functionally, and the incumbent brick/teal does exactly the same thing (#B44959 vs #35606F). Tritanopia is also ~0.01% and not sex-linked, where the brief’s red-green concern is ~8% of boys — so this is the right corner to lose in.
  • Second weakest — blue vs the quiz teal Blueprint blue is ΔE₀₀ 21.4 from the quiz stock #2F6360. Clearly distinct (the teal is half the chroma and 73° away in hue) and the two never appear on the same card — a fork card has no teal on it — but across a scroll session they are the closest adjacency this proposal creates. Pushing the blue toward indigo would fix it, at the cost of protanopia separation and of a more saturated, less period blue. The trade was taken deliberately.
  • The ink ledge Every ticket keeps its 3px ink border and its chunky ink ledge. Mulberry sits at 1.60:1 against #1C3B2E, marginally under the teal it replaces (1.79:1), so the ledge under choice B is a slightly softer step than before. Blueprint blue is 2.23:1, which is a hair under brick’s 2.37:1 and comfortably the strongest ledge on the card. If B’s ledge ever reads flat, lighten it to #7D4B64 (1.79:1, exactly the teal it replaces) and accept protanopia 14.8.
  • Nothing else moves Teal stays the quiz stock, gold stays the single-path “next” ticket, sage stays the default keyline. Three hex values change. All solid backgroundColor / borderColor — no gradients, no blend modes on these swatches, React-Native-portable as-is.
