A value-led pair: lightness does the talking, hue only confirms it. A deep drafting-blue against a chalky heather twenty L* points lighter — 21 points of lightness separation survives every form of colour blindness, because no deficiency touches luminance. Red and green are gone from the fork entirely; the retired --brick #B24F35 is not reused anywhere.

Why it says "different", never "right vs wrong"

Valence in a two-colour pair comes from the traffic-light hues — a fork that is red against green is read as a marked answer sheet, not as two roads. Blueprint and heather are both cool, chromatic and blameless: one is the drafting ink of a plan, the other the chalk-dusted lilac of a 1950s classroom wall, and neither has ever meant stop or go.

The difference between them is carried by lightness, not by hue-warmth — a deep thing and a pale thing, which the eye files as "two distances / two depths of the same journey" rather than as "the good one and the bad one". Depth is a direction; warmth is a judgement.

The four things that had to be proved

  • The real risk of a value pair — "one live, one dead" A dark button beside a pale button is exactly how every UI kit draws enabled vs disabled. Four rules kill that read, and they are the whole design: (1) identical furniture — same 48px min-height, same 3px forest-ink border, same 4px ink ledge, same 12px radius, same 26px cream key chip, same 17px cream word; (2) chevron opacity 1 on both (the original fork used .85 — anything dimmed on one row nominates the other); (3) both are chromatic — heather carries C* 16, the same chroma band as the palette's teal (18) and olive (16). Disabled states are grey and low-contrast; heather is neither; (4) both clear 4.5:1, so neither label whispers.
  • Both branch cards fan out, in their own colour .fan-a and .fan-b are the two real next-cards showing behind the deck, one tinted blueprint, one tinted heather. Two solid coloured cards, equal size, equal rotation — the fork is a physical split in the deck, not a question with an answer. In production, which colour lands on A vs B is randomised per fork, so neither hue can accumulate a meaning across a session.
  • The banner is a third, impartial colour "SANGANDAAN", its rule, and the card keyline were --brick — the same colour as choice A, which quietly nominated A. They are now graphite #4B4641, a near-neutral warm grey (C* 3.9): ΔE00 22.7 from blueprint and 19.3 from heather — near-equidistant, a member of neither camp, and it sits between them in value (L* 30 against 22 and 43) like a signpost between two roads. Why not reuse --olive? Olive is L* 43.3 — value-identical to heather, so it would have tied the banner to B by lightness instead of by hue. Graphite also lifts the keyline from brick's 4.33:1 to 7.81:1 on cream.
  • Teal keeps its day job --teal #2F6360 is not repurposed: the quiz page stays teal stock with its gold keyline, exactly as in the parent mock-up. Only --brick is retired, and it is retired outright — it appears nowhere in this file. Honest count: the palette goes from ten colours to twelve — brick out, blueprint + heather + graphite in. That is the cost of the fix, and it is the right cost: brick was doing double duty as the banner and as choice A, which is precisely the bug. A fork needs three colours: two options and one impartial signpost.
  • Method — no eyeballing CVD simulation is Brettel–Viénot–Mollon (1997), the two-half-plane projection applied in linear sRGB with the libDaltonLens coefficients (dichromat, i.e. 100% severity — the worst case, not an anomalous-trichromat softening). Distances are CIEDE2000 with CIE76 alongside, computed in CIELAB under D65. Contrast is WCAG 2.1 relative luminance. Every number in the strip is machine-computed from the final hexes, not estimated.
  • Honest — the weakest point Heather is 4.82:1. That is a pass, but only 7% of headroom, and it is a hard ceiling: cream-on-colour at 4.5:1 caps the lighter member at about L* 44, which is what bounds the value split at ~21 L*. If the label ever drops below 17px bold or the panel is worse than assumed, heather is the first thing to fail — the fix is to darken it a step (L* 40, #6B5669, 5.6:1) and accept a slightly smaller split. Second: at L* 22 blueprint is the same value as the forest-ink border, so on choice A the printed border merges into the fill and the button silhouette is carried by the cream stock around it. Deliberate — the alternative was a lighter blue and a smaller value split — but it does mean choice A reads as a solid slab and choice B as an outlined chip. Under tritanopia (rare) blueprint also collapses toward the forest ink, #143A45 vs #223840; the two choices stay 26.4 apart, which is what the fork depends on.
  • Still mid-century, still the cat's world Blueprint is drafting-linen blue and heather is dusty lilac — both 1950s schoolroom colours, both low-chroma (C* 31 and 16 against gold's 60 and the old brick's 52; this pair is quieter than what it replaces). They are cool, so they sit behind the cat's olive/forest/golden-eye warmth instead of competing with it, and neither comes near the gold ticket (ΔE00 64.4 and 44.9). No gradients, no blend modes, no opacity tricks on any swatch — four solid backgroundColor values, React-Native-portable as-is.
